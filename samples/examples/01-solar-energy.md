<!-- title: Solar energy -->
Global solar capacity has grown faster than any other energy source over the past decade.

By the end of 2024, worldwide installed photovoltaic capacity had [reached 2,200 gigawatts](https://www.iea.org/reports/renewables-2024-nonexistent-page), and [China alone accounted for more than 60%](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/this-article-does-not-exist-404) of all new installations that year.

The levelised cost of solar electricity [fell by 89% between 2010 and 2023](https://ourworldindata.org/energy), which is why utility-scale projects now outcompete new gas capacity across most of the world.

Efficiency has improved in parallel, and silicon-based panels [now routinely exceed 45% efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cell_efficiency) in commercial deployment.

Solar power [accounted for about 5.5% of global electricity generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power) in 2023, up from well under 1% a decade earlier.

A [2024 industry review](https://global-energy-review.org/2024/solar-outlook) projected that installations would triple again by 2027.
